Let's begin.

Number one. Micellar water.

If you have ever looked in a French woman's bathroom, there is a good chance you have seen a clear bottle of micellar water sitting next to a stack of soft round cotton pads. It looks like plain water, but it is designed to lift away makeup, sunscreen, and the day's grime without a lot of rubbing and without needing to rinse.

Why it works after sixty. Our skin often becomes drier and more delicate as we age, and harsh foaming cleansers can leave it feeling tight and stripped. Micellar water is a gentle way to cleanse, especially at night when you are tired and do not want a big production at the sink. It is also wonderful for the delicate area around the eyes, where so many of us have a little mascara to remove.

How to use it. Soak a cotton pad, press it gently against your closed eye for a few seconds to let the makeup soften, and then sweep it away gently. Use a fresh pad for the rest of the face. Many women follow it with a splash of water or a gentle cleanser, especially if they wear a lot of makeup or sunscreen. Picture a white marble sink, a little glass dish of cotton pads, and a soft linen towel. That is the whole ritual.

The mistake to avoid. Scrubbing. The point of micellar water is to be gentle. If you are rubbing back and forth hard, especially around the eyes, you are working against your skin. Let the pad sit, let it do its job, and use a light hand.

Number two. The cleansing milk or cream cleanser.

Before foaming gels became so popular, many French women grew up with cleansing milk. Picture a soft white lotion in a simple bottle, poured into the palm, massaged over the face, and then wiped away with a warm, damp washcloth or rinsed with lukewarm water.

Why it works after sixty. A creamy cleanser feels cushioning rather than stripping. For many of us, our skin produces less oil than it used to, and a milk cleanser respects that. My skin simply feels more comfortable after a cleansing milk than it ever did after a foaming wash.

How to use it. Warm a small amount between your fingertips and massage it into dry skin in slow circles. This little massage is a lovely moment in itself. Then remove it with a soft, warm washcloth, pressing gently rather than dragging. Follow with your moisturizer while your skin is still a tiny bit damp.

The mistake to avoid. Using water that is too hot. I know a hot washcloth feels wonderful on a cold night, but very hot water can leave mature skin feeling dry and flushed. Lukewarm is your friend.

Hold on to that number nine, by the way. We are getting closer, and I promise it is worth the wait.

Number three. Thermal spring water mist.

This is one of those things that I always found a little puzzling until I tried it. Picture a slim aerosol can, often silver or white, sitting on a vanity or tucked into a handbag. You hold it a little distance from your face and mist a fine, cool spray of water over your skin.

Why it works after sixty. For many women, it is simply soothing. On a hot summer day, after a long flight, or right after cleansing, a cool mist feels refreshing and calming. It is also a nice step before moisturizer, because applying cream to slightly damp skin helps it spread easily and feel more comfortable.

How to use it. After cleansing, mist your face lightly, wait just a moment, and then pat in your serum or moisturizer. In summer, keep a small can in your bag and use it over your makeup when you feel warm. Picture a woman on a sunny terrace in a white linen shirt and a straw hat, giving her face a quick mist before she sips her iced tea.

The mistake to avoid. Letting it evaporate completely on bare skin and leaving it at that. On its own, water can evaporate and leave your skin feeling drier than before. Always follow a mist with a moisturizer or a facial oil to seal in that comfort.

Number four. The rich, simple moisturizer.

Here is where the French pharmacy approach really differs from the department store. Instead of a heavy glass jar with a gold lid and a long list of fancy promises, picture a plain white tube or a simple pot of thick, rich cream, often fragrance-free, with a short, straightforward label that talks about comfort and dryness.

Why it works after sixty. As our skin becomes drier and more sensitive, many of us do better with fewer ingredients and less fragrance. A rich, simple cream can make your face feel soft and comfortable all day, without the stinging some heavily perfumed products cause. I found that when I simplified, my skin felt calmer.

How to use it. Warm a pea-sized amount between your fingers, then press it into your cheeks, forehead, and chin, and smooth it outward. Do not forget your neck and the top of your chest, where the skin is just as delicate. Many women use a lighter version in summer and a richer one in winter.

The mistake to avoid. Thinking more expensive always means better. A beautiful jar can make us feel special, and there is nothing wrong with that. But when it comes to how your skin actually feels, pay attention to comfort, not packaging. If a cream stings or makes you red, stop using it, no matter how lovely the box was.

Number five. Daily sunscreen.

If there is one habit that I see in nearly every thoughtful skincare routine, it is sunscreen every single morning. Picture a small white tube on the windowsill next to a bottle of moisturizer, and a woman in a striped Breton top applying a thin layer before she heads out for her walk.

Why it works after sixty. Our skin has seen a lifetime of sun. Many of us grew up when nobody wore sunscreen, and we spent summers at the beach with baby oil. Dermatologists widely recommend daily sun protection for everyone, and I take that advice seriously. It is also the step that makes me feel I am actually caring for my skin, not just covering it.

How to use it. Apply it as the last step of your morning skincare, before makeup. Cover your face, your ears, your neck, and the backs of your hands. If you will be outside for a long time, follow the directions on the label for reapplying. Pair it with a wide-brimmed straw hat and a good pair of sunglasses on bright days, and you have a very French, very chic approach to sun care.

The mistake to avoid. Skipping it on cloudy days or in winter. It is easy to think you do not need it when the sky is gray, but daylight is still there. Make it as automatic as brushing your teeth. And if you are unsure which type is right for your skin, ask your dermatologist or your pharmacist.

Number six. The hand cream ritual.

I have noticed that French women seem to treat their hands as part of their skincare, not as an afterthought. Picture a small tube of hand cream in a coat pocket, another one on the kitchen windowsill, another one on the nightstand. A woman in a navy cardigan, pausing after washing dishes, smoothing cream into her knuckles and fingertips.

Why it works after sixty. Our hands show so much of our life. They do so much, and they are washed constantly, which can leave them dry and rough. A simple, rich hand cream helps them feel soft and cared for, and soft, well-kept hands are one of the most elegant details a woman can have, along with neat, short nails.

How to use it. Apply after every hand washing, if you can, and always before bed. At night, try a thicker layer and slip on a pair of soft cotton gloves for a few hours. Massage it into your cuticles too. It takes thirty seconds and it feels like a little gift to yourself.

The mistake to avoid. Keeping only one tube in the bathroom. If the cream is not where you are, you will not use it. Put a tube in every room you spend time in, and in your handbag.

Number seven. The lip balm, always.

This is such a small thing, but it is part of that polished French look. Picture a simple little tube of lip balm, maybe in a red and white or a plain white package, sitting in the pocket of a camel trench coat.

Why it works after sixty. Our lips can become thinner and drier over time, and dry, cracked lips can make the whole face look tired. A good balm keeps them soft, and it also makes lipstick go on more smoothly. Soft lips with a sheer wash of rose or berry color are one of the simplest ways to look fresh.

How to use it. Apply a balm in the morning, let it sink in while you do the rest of your routine, then add your lipstick if you wear one. Reapply through the day. At night, apply a thicker layer before bed.

The mistake to avoid. Licking your lips when they feel dry. It is a habit so many of us have, and it tends to make dryness worse. Keep a balm with you instead.

Now, number eight is a gentle one, and then I promise we get to the habit that surprised me most.

Number eight. The simple eye cream, applied with the ring finger.

Picture a tiny tube or a small pot of eye cream, and a woman in a soft white bathrobe gently tapping it around the outer corners of her eyes with her ring finger.

Why it works after sixty. The skin around the eyes is very thin and delicate, and many of us find that our regular face cream feels too heavy or a little irritating there. A gentle eye cream made for that area can feel more comfortable. Some women simply use a small amount of their gentle, fragrance-free moisturizer instead, and that is fine too. What matters most is the gentle touch.

How to use it. Take a tiny amount, about the size of a grain of rice for both eyes. Use your ring finger, because it naturally applies the lightest pressure, and tap it along the bone around the eye. Never pull or drag the skin.

The mistake to avoid. Using too much, or applying it too close to the lash line. A heavy layer can migrate into the eyes and cause irritation. Less really is more here.

Number nine. The facial massage.

Yes, it is that simple. Not a product, not a gadget, just your own two hands. I first noticed it when I read how many French women talk about massaging their creams into their skin rather than just slapping them on. Picture a woman sitting at a small wooden vanity in the evening light, a cream-colored silk robe, a little dish of face oil, slowly sweeping her fingertips from the center of her face outward, along her jawline, up over her cheekbones, and across her forehead.

Why it works after sixty. For me, it is about how my face feels and looks the next morning. When I take two or three minutes to massage my moisturizer in slowly, my face simply feels more relaxed, and I seem to look a little less puffy and tired when I wake up. I am not going to promise you that it changes your skin in any medical way, because I am not qualified to say that. But I can tell you it is one of the most calming rituals in my day, and it makes my skin feel cared for.

How to use it. After cleansing, warm a little cream or a few drops of facial oil between your palms. Press your palms over your face for a moment and breathe. Then, with gentle pressure, sweep from your chin along your jaw to your ears. Then from the sides of your nose out across your cheekbones. Then from between your eyebrows up and out across your forehead. Finish by gently pressing your fingertips along your neck, moving downward. Two minutes, every night.

The mistake to avoid. Pressing too hard or pulling the skin. This should feel soothing, not like a workout. If anything feels uncomfortable, stop. And if you have any skin condition, ask your doctor before you start.

If that one surprised you, wait until you hear number eleven. It is the habit that ties this whole approach together.

Number ten. The body cream or oil after the bath.

French pharmacy shelves always seem to have rows of body creams in simple, oversized bottles. Picture a large white pump bottle on the edge of a clawfoot bathtub, a fluffy white towel, and a woman smoothing a rich lotion over her arms and legs while her skin is still slightly damp.

Why it works after sixty. The skin on our bodies can become drier as we age too, especially on the legs, elbows, and shins. A simple daily habit of body cream after a shower keeps skin feeling soft and comfortable. And if you are wearing those beautiful cropped trousers and midi skirts we talk about on this channel, smooth, moisturized ankles and calves make a lovely finishing touch.

How to use it. Pat yourself dry gently, leaving your skin a little damp, and then apply your body cream or oil right away. Pay extra attention to elbows, knees, heels, and shins. In winter, many women switch to something richer.

The mistake to avoid. Long, very hot showers. They feel wonderful, but they can leave skin feeling parched. Keep showers warm and reasonably short, and moisturize right after.

Number eleven. Less, but consistently.

This is the idea I promised you, and it ties everything together. The French pharmacy approach is not about owning dozens of products. It is about choosing a few simple, gentle things, and using them every single day. Picture a small bathroom shelf with just five or six items: a cleansing milk, a micellar water, a mist, a moisturizer, a sunscreen, and a hand cream. That is it.

Why it works after sixty. Our skin often becomes more reactive as we age, and layering many products, especially strong ones with lots of active ingredients, can sometimes lead to irritation. A short, simple routine is easier on the skin and so much easier to keep up. And honestly, after a lifetime of busy mornings, there is real peace in a routine that takes five minutes.

How to use it. In the morning, cleanse gently or just rinse with water, mist if you like, moisturize, and finish with sunscreen. In the evening, remove makeup with micellar water, cleanse with a milk, mist, and massage in your moisturizer. Add your hand cream and lip balm before bed. If you want to try a new product, introduce only one at a time, and test it on a small patch of skin first, like the inside of your arm, for a few days.

The mistake to avoid. Chasing every new trend. It is so tempting when we see the latest miracle in an advertisement. But changing your routine constantly makes it hard to know what is actually working. Find what feels good, and stay with it.
