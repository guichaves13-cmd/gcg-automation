It is late afternoon in a small Paris square. The light is turning gold, there are pigeons near the fountain, and a woman with silver hair cut into a soft bob is crossing the cobblestones in a long navy wool coat. Underneath, a cream cable-knit sweater and a pair of camel trousers. Around her neck, a silk scarf in rust and soft blue. Not one piece of black on her. And she glows. That is the only word I have for it. The colors around her face catch the light, and she looks rested, warm, and completely herself.

For most of my adult life, I lived in black. Black trousers, black sweaters, black dresses, black coats. I thought it was slimming, I thought it was sophisticated, and I thought it was the safest choice for a woman who did not want to make a mistake. And for a long time, it worked for me. But somewhere in my sixties, I started to notice that black near my face made me look tired. My skin looked a little gray, the shadows under my eyes looked deeper, and my silver hair looked flat instead of bright. I did not feel tired. But I looked it.

So here is my promise to you today. I am going to share eleven colors and color ideas that so many elegant French women over sixty seem to wear instead of head-to-toe black, and that I have come to love. For each one, I will tell you why it works so well after sixty, how to style it, and the one mistake to avoid. And I am not telling you to throw out every black thing you own. By the end, I will show you how to keep black in your wardrobe, just in a smarter place.

And stay with me, because the eighth one is the color that surprised me most. I would never have picked it for myself, and now it is the one I get the most compliments on.

Let's begin.

Number one. Navy, the new black.

If you take away only one idea from this video, let it be this one. Picture a deep navy wool blazer, a navy cashmere crew-neck, a pair of navy trousers with a crisp crease. Navy is the color I see over and over again on stylish French women, and it does almost everything black does, but more gently.

Why it works after sixty. Navy is still dark, so it gives you that same grounded, polished, slimming feeling you loved about black. But it has warmth and depth, and it is softer against mature skin. Next to the face, navy tends to look fresh where black can look severe. It also works beautifully with silver and gray hair, which can look flat against black.

How to style it. A navy sweater with dark straight jeans and white sneakers for a weekday. A navy blazer over a striped Breton top with cream trousers and tan loafers for lunch with friends. A navy wrap dress with a pair of pearl earrings and nude flats for a dinner out. Navy pairs with almost everything, cream, camel, gray, red, and soft pink.

The mistake to avoid. Choosing a navy so dark it reads as black. Some navies are nearly black under indoor light, and then you lose the softening effect. Hold the fabric next to something black in daylight. You want to see the blue clearly.

Number two. Camel, the color of quiet luxury.

Picture a long camel wool coat with a wide lapel, a camel cashmere turtleneck, a pair of camel wide-leg trousers. This warm, golden tan is one of the most elegant colors a woman can wear, and it is a staple of French style.

Why it works after sixty. Camel is warm, and warmth near the face is very kind to mature skin. It brings a gentle glow, it softens lines, and it looks wonderful with silver, blonde, and gray hair. It also looks expensive, even when it is not, because it suggests fine wool and cashmere.

How to style it. A camel coat over a cream sweater and dark denim with brown leather ankle boots is one of the most classic French looks there is. Or try camel trousers with a navy sweater and a navy and white striped scarf. If you want to feel very polished, go head to toe in camel and cream, with gold earrings and cognac loafers.

One more thought on camel. It is a wonderful color for outerwear in particular. A camel coat is something many French women seem to keep for years and years, pulling it out every fall, and it works over almost anything else in the closet, navy, gray, cream, brown, even black trousers. If you are going to invest in one coat that is not black, I would gently suggest camel or navy. Picture yourself walking out on a crisp October morning, leaves underfoot, a camel coat belted at the waist and a soft gray scarf at your neck. It feels like a little bit of Paris, wherever you live.

The mistake to avoid. Choosing a camel that matches your skin too closely. If the color is almost the same as your complexion, it can wash you out. If that happens, add a scarf in a contrasting color near your face, or choose a camel that is a little deeper or a little more golden.

Keep that number eight in mind. The color that surprised me most is coming, and I think it might surprise you too.

Number three. Cream and ivory instead of stark white.

Picture a soft cream cable-knit sweater, an ivory silk blouse with a gentle bow, a pair of winter white wool trousers. These are the creamy, slightly warm whites that French women seem to wear all year round, not just in summer.

Why it works after sixty. Bright, stark white can be a little harsh next to mature skin and silver hair, and it can make teeth and skin look more yellow by contrast. Cream and ivory are softer. They reflect light up onto the face like a little reflector, which brightens the complexion without any harshness. And a cream sweater simply looks luxurious.

How to style it. A cream sweater with camel trousers and a camel coat is a beautiful tonal look. An ivory blouse with navy trousers and navy ballet flats is crisp and classic. In winter, try winter white wool trousers with a cream turtleneck and a long gray coat. It feels so fresh on a dark day.

The mistake to avoid. Worrying too much about stains and never wearing it. I used to avoid light colors for that reason. But cream that is cared for, hand washed, gently steamed, kept away from your morning coffee, can last for years. Treat it with a little respect and enjoy it.

Number four. Soft heather gray.

Picture a heather gray cashmere sweater with a crew neck, a pair of light gray flannel trousers, a long gray knit cardigan. Not a cold, flat gray, but a soft, slightly mottled gray, the color of a rainy Paris sky.

Why it works after sixty. Gray is the color that many of us are wearing on our heads now, and when you wear a soft gray near your face, it creates a beautiful harmony with silver hair. It is calm and sophisticated. It is also an easy step away from black, because it still feels neutral and quiet.

How to style it. A heather gray sweater with dark jeans and a pair of burgundy loafers. Light gray flannel trousers with a cream blouse and a navy blazer. A long gray cardigan over a white tee and navy trousers. Add a pair of silver hoops, and the whole look feels effortless.

The mistake to avoid. Wearing gray in a flat, dull fabric with nothing to lift it. Gray needs texture, a cable knit, a soft brushed flannel, a slubby linen, or a pop of color nearby. Otherwise it can look a bit gloomy.

Now, the next few colors are where things get really interesting, because they are the ones most of us never think to try. Number five. Chocolate brown.

Picture a chocolate brown suede jacket, a brown wool turtleneck, a pair of deep brown wide-leg trousers. Brown has been making a quiet return, and I think French women never really let it go.

Why it works after sixty. Chocolate brown gives you the depth and seriousness of black, but with warmth. It is much softer next to the face, and it pairs beautifully with gold jewelry, warm skin tones, and silver hair. It feels rich and grounded without any harshness.

How to style it. A chocolate brown turtleneck with cream trousers and tan loafers. Brown wide-leg trousers with a cream silk blouse and a gold belt. A brown suede jacket over a navy and white striped tee with dark jeans. Brown and navy together is a combination I see often on stylish women, and it always looks quietly sophisticated.

Brown also works beautifully in accessories. A cognac leather handbag, a tortoiseshell pair of glasses, a brown suede belt. These small warm touches soften an outfit that might otherwise feel cold. If you are not ready for a brown sweater, start with a brown bag, and notice how much warmer your whole look feels.

The mistake to avoid. Pairing brown with black. Those two together can look muddy and heavy. If you love brown, let it partner with cream, camel, navy, or soft blue instead.

If brown felt like a gentle step, this next one is a little brighter, and it is one of my favorites for spring. Number six. Soft blues near the face.

Picture a pale blue cotton shirt with the collar open, a powder blue cashmere sweater, a silk scarf in chambray and white. These gentle, airy blues are wonderful for women with silver hair and fair or pink-toned skin.

Why it works after sixty. Light blue brightens the face. It brings out blue and gray eyes beautifully, it looks fresh and clean, and it has a calm, relaxed quality. It is also very easy to wear, because it goes with denim, navy, white, cream, and gray.

How to style it. A pale blue shirt tucked into cream trousers with white sneakers for a spring walk. A powder blue sweater with gray flannel trousers and a navy coat in winter. A blue and white scarf knotted loosely at the neck of a navy blazer. It is a little bit of sky right next to your face.

The mistake to avoid. Choosing a blue that is too bright or too electric. Neon or very saturated blues can overpower a mature complexion. Look for blues that look soft, a little dusty, or a little gray.

We are one step away from the color that surprised me most. But first, one that feels like a glass of red wine by the fire. Number seven. Burgundy and wine.

Picture a burgundy cashmere sweater, a wine-colored silk blouse, a pair of deep oxblood leather loafers. This rich red-brown color is one of the most elegant shades a woman can wear in autumn and winter.

Why it works after sixty. Burgundy has the depth of a dark color but with a beautiful warmth that brings color back into the cheeks and lips. It is much more flattering near the face than black for many women, and it looks rich and mature in the best possible way.

How to style it. A burgundy sweater with gray flannel trousers and black, or better, oxblood loafers. A wine silk blouse with navy trousers and gold earrings for an evening out. If a whole burgundy top feels like too much, start with the shoes or a handbag, and repeat the color in a scarf or a lipstick.

The mistake to avoid. Pairing it with a lipstick that clashes. Burgundy sings with a berry, rose, or wine lip, but can look odd next to a bright coral or orange. Keep your lip color in the same family.

Alright, here it is. You stayed with me, and I promised you the color that surprised me most.

Number eight. Soft dusty pink.

I never, ever thought I would wear pink. I thought pink was for little girls or for women who wanted to look sweet. But then I saw a woman, I would guess in her seventies, at a flower market, in a dusty rose cashmere sweater with gray trousers and a long camel coat. Silver hair, small pearl earrings, a bunch of white tulips in her arms. She looked radiant. Not sweet, not girlish, just warm and alive.

Why it works after sixty. A soft, dusty pink, the color of dried roses or a pale blush, reflects a rosy warmth back onto the face. For many of us, as we age, our skin loses some of its natural color, and a gentle pink near the face can bring some of that freshness back. It is wonderful with silver and gray hair, and it is surprisingly versatile.

How to style it. A dusty pink sweater with gray flannel trousers and a camel coat, just like that woman at the flower market. A blush silk blouse under a navy blazer with dark jeans. A soft pink scarf with a cream sweater and camel trousers. If you are nervous about it, start with a scarf. See how it looks in the mirror in daylight. I think you will be surprised.

The mistake to avoid. Choosing a pink that is too bright, too sugary, or too bubblegum. The magic is in the dusty, muted, slightly grayed shades. And keep the rest of the outfit simple and grown-up, gray, camel, navy, cream. That is what keeps pink from looking sweet.

And if that one surprised you, wait for number eleven, because it is the trick that lets you keep loving black without letting it age you.

Two more colors and two clever ideas left, and the very last one is the one I use every single day. Number nine. Olive and soft khaki.

Picture an olive green utility jacket, a khaki cotton trench, a pair of olive wide-leg trousers. These earthy greens are very much a part of relaxed French weekend dressing.

Why it works after sixty. Olive is a wonderful neutral that feels casual and easy, but it has more life than black. It pairs beautifully with warm skin tones and with camel, cream, and navy. It feels practical and outdoorsy without ever looking sloppy.

How to style it. An olive jacket over a cream sweater and dark jeans with brown ankle boots. Olive wide-leg trousers with a white linen shirt and tan sandals in summer. A khaki trench over a navy and white striped top with cream trousers in spring.

Olive is also a lovely color for travel. It hides little marks from a long day, it pairs with almost everything you might pack, and it looks relaxed in a train station or a country market. Picture an olive jacket, a cream knit, dark jeans, and white sneakers, with a straw tote over your shoulder. That is an outfit you could wear from breakfast to dinner.

The mistake to avoid. Wearing olive right against the face if it makes you look sallow. For some complexions, olive can bring out a yellowish tone. If you notice that, keep the olive on the bottom or on an outer jacket, and put cream or a soft blue nearest your face.

Now, here is an idea rather than a single shade, and it is the one that makes everything else feel effortless. Number ten. Tonal dressing.

This is not a single color, but an idea that French women use so well. Tonal dressing means wearing several shades of the same color family together. Picture cream trousers, an ivory sweater, and a camel coat. Or navy trousers, a powder blue shirt, and a navy blazer. Or a gray flannel trouser, a heather gray sweater, and a charcoal coat.

Why it works after sixty. Tonal dressing gives you the same long, unbroken, elegant line that made black so appealing, but in a much softer palette. It looks effortless and expensive, and it is very forgiving, because there are no harsh color breaks cutting the body in half.

How to style it. Pick a color family, cream and camel, navy and blue, or gray and silver, and build an outfit with three different shades of it. Then add one small accent, a gold earring, a red lip, a tan leather bag. Mixing textures helps too, a chunky knit with a smooth trouser, a silk blouse with a wool coat.

The mistake to avoid. Using exactly the same shade in the same fabric from head to toe. That can look like a uniform. The beauty of tonal dressing is in the small variations of shade and texture.

Number eleven. Keep black, but move it away from your face.

This is the trick I promised you. I still own black. I still love it. But I have moved it. Picture a pair of black wool trousers worn with a cream sweater. A black leather handbag carried with a camel coat. Black ankle boots under navy jeans. A black skirt with a dusty pink knit.

Why it works after sixty. The part of black that can be hard on mature skin is when it sits right next to the face, casting shadows. When you wear black on the lower half of the body, in shoes, in bags, or in a belt, you keep all its strength and sophistication, while the colors closest to your face do the brightening work.

How to style it. Black trousers with a soft gray sweater and silver earrings. A black dress with a cream cardigan over the shoulders and a pale blue scarf. A black coat with a camel or rust scarf wrapped around the neck. Even a simple strand of pearls or a white collar peeking out over a black sweater can make a big difference.

The mistake to avoid. Thinking it is all or nothing. You do not have to give up black completely. You just have to give your face a little light. One scarf, one cream collar, one soft sweater can change everything.

So let's bring it all together. Here are the eleven ideas we covered today. Number one, navy, the new black. Number two, camel. Number three, cream and ivory. Number four, soft heather gray. Number five, chocolate brown. Number six, soft blues near the face. Number seven, burgundy and wine. Number eight, dusty pink, the one that surprised me most. Number nine, olive and khaki. Number ten, tonal dressing. And number eleven, keeping black, but moving it away from your face.

Here is what I want you to remember. Black is not the enemy. It served so many of us beautifully for years. But our coloring changes as we age, our hair softens to silver, our skin becomes more delicate, and the colors that flatter us change too. French women of a certain age seem to understand this instinctively. They choose colors that bring light to the face, warmth to the skin, and softness to the whole look.

And remember, color does not have to arrive all at once. You can begin with the smallest touches. A pale blue scarf. A dusty pink lipstick. A cream collar peeking out over a navy sweater. A pair of cognac loafers instead of black ones. Each small change brings a little more light into your wardrobe, and before long you will notice that you reach for black less and less, simply because the other colors make you feel so good.

Try this. Tomorrow morning, stand near a window in natural daylight. Hold a black sweater under your chin, then a navy one, then something cream, then a soft pink or a pale blue. Look at your skin, your eyes, the shadows under them. You will see it right away. One of those colors will make you look rested and bright. That is your new starting point.

And when you find that color, wear it with confidence. Wear it the way that woman crossed the square in her navy coat and cream sweater, with her rust and blue scarf catching the gold afternoon light. Not trying to look younger. Just looking alive, and looking like herself.
