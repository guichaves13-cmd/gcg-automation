But first, let's talk about what we're switching away from.

Polyester and synthetic blends.

I want to be fair. Synthetic fabrics have a place. Some modern blends are well made, they are useful for sportswear, rainwear and certain kinds of travel. I'm not here to tell you every synthetic piece is bad.

But many of us, over the years, have filled our closets with polyester blouses, synthetic knit tops, stretchy blended trousers and polyester dresses, mostly because they're easy. They don't wrinkle, they dry quickly, they're inexpensive. And as we get older, I think many of us start to notice what they cost us in other ways.

Let me tell you what I've noticed in my own body and on the women around me.

Synthetic fabrics often don't breathe the way natural fibers do. On a warm day, or in a heated room, they can hold heat and moisture against the skin. Many women I know, especially as our bodies change in our fifties and sixties, tell me they feel hot and uncomfortable in synthetics, especially at night or during warm spells. Natural fibers often feel cooler and more comfortable to them.

Then there's the look. Many polyester fabrics have a particular shine, a hard, slightly plastic gleam, that the eye tends to read as inexpensive. They often cling, especially to the back, the arms and the tummy, showing every line. They can pill, especially where the arms rub against the body. And many of them collect static, so they stick to tights or lift away from the body at strange moments.

And finally, there's the feeling. When I touch a polyester blouse, it feels slippery and cool for a moment, then warm and a little sticky. When I touch linen, it feels dry, cool and alive. That difference in feeling, I've come to believe, changes how we move and how we carry ourselves.

Piece number one. The linen shirt.

If you only own one linen piece, make it this one. A simple linen shirt, with a collar, buttons down the front, and long sleeves you can roll to the elbow. Slightly relaxed in fit, not tight, not enormous.

Why does a linen shirt work so beautifully after sixty? First, it breathes. Linen allows air to move through it, so it feels cool in summer and comfortable in heated rooms in winter. Second, the collar near the face acts like a frame, lifting and brightening. Third, linen has a slightly irregular texture, with tiny slubs and variations in the weave, that catches the light softly rather than shining harshly. And fourth, a linen shirt drapes over the body without clinging, so it's flattering on almost every figure.

Piece number two. Linen trousers.

Compared to synthetic stretch trousers, which can cling to the thighs and bag at the knees, linen trousers fall straight and stay away from the body. They forgive. They flatter. And they let you sit on a terrace for three hours without feeling like your clothes are glued to you.

Piece number four. The linen blazer.

Piece number six. The linen scarf.

A linen scarf is the simplest, smallest, most underestimated piece in this whole video. And yet I think it might be the one that changes how you feel the most.

A long, lightweight linen scarf, slightly frayed or hemmed at the edges, in a solid color, a soft stripe, or a simple print. Worn loosely around the neck, knotted at the side, draped over the shoulders, or wrapped around the head on a windy day.

Why linen for a scarf? Because a silk scarf is beautiful, but it can be slippery and warm. A wool scarf is cozy, but too heavy for much of the year. A synthetic scarf can feel scratchy or create static. A linen scarf is cool, light, soft, and it holds a knot without sliding. It protects the neck from the sun in summer and from a breeze in spring and autumn. And because it sits right next to the face, it brings color and texture exactly where we need it most.

Italian women seem to own linen scarves in every color, but the ones I see most often are sea blue, terracotta, olive, natural, and soft stripes. They wear them with everything, a linen shirt, a T-shirt, a blazer, a dress, a coat.

And now, the ritual I promised. How Italian women make linen look elegant instead of messy.

Because I know what you're thinking. Linen wrinkles. Won't I look crumpled? Here's what I've learned from watching the women who wear it best.

First, wash it well. Linen likes gentle washing and cool water, and it gets softer with every wash. Many women I know wash it at home and avoid the dryer when they can, because high heat can shrink it and set hard creases.

Second, hang it while it's still damp. Shake it out firmly, smooth the collar and cuffs with your hands, and hang it on a good hanger. Many of the creases simply fall out as it dries.

Third, iron once, or steam. If you want a crisp look, iron linen while it's still slightly damp, on a fairly hot setting. If you prefer a softer look, simply hang it in a steamy bathroom for a few minutes.

Fourth, then let it live. This is the most important step of all. Once you put it on, don't fight it. Linen will crease where you bend, at the elbows, the knees, the waist. Those creases are not a sign of carelessness. They're the signature of the fabric. In Italy, I've observed, a softly creased linen shirt is considered relaxed and elegant, while a perfectly stiff, wrinkle-free synthetic can seem a little lifeless.

And fifth, choose quality where you can. Heavier, denser linen tends to wrinkle more softly than very thin linen, and it lasts longer. Linen blended with a little cotton can soften the creasing even further while keeping the breathability.

Let me give you a few practical tips for beginning your own switch.

First, don't throw everything away at once. Start by replacing the synthetic pieces you wear most often, the summer blouse, the everyday trousers, the go-to dress.

Second, start with a linen shirt in a color that flatters your face. White, ivory, sky blue or sand are wonderful places to begin.

Third, look at labels. Many garments are sold as linen but are mostly synthetic with a small amount of linen added. Look for pieces that are mostly or entirely linen, or linen with cotton.

Fourth, buy fewer, better pieces. A good linen shirt can last for many years and become more beautiful with time.

And fifth, wear it with confidence. Linen looks best on a woman who's not worried about it. Put it on, roll up the sleeves, and forget about the creases.
