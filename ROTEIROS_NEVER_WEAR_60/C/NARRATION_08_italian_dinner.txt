First, why does dinner matter so much?

In Italy, dinner isn't just eating. It's the event of the day. It's where families gather, friends meet, stories are told, arguments are settled and started again. It often begins late and lasts for hours. And dressing for dinner isn't about showing off. It's a gesture of respect, for the food, for the people who made it, for the people at the table. When an Italian woman over sixty changes her clothes before dinner, she's saying, this moment matters. And that, I've come to believe, is one of the most beautiful habits we can borrow.

Number one. The fleece jacket.

We've talked about fleece before on this channel, but at dinner, it deserves special attention. The fleece jacket is warm, light and practical, and it's become the default layer for so many of us, especially when we travel.

But at a dinner table, fleece has several problems. Under warm restaurant lighting, its flat, synthetic surface absorbs light and looks dull. It has no structure, so the shoulders slope. The zip front and the stand-up collar are sporty, made for mountain paths, not for candlelit tables. And when you sit, fleece bunches around the middle, adding bulk exactly where none of us want it.

What do Italian women wear instead? A silk shirt.

A simple silk shirt, slightly loose, with a soft collar or a gentle V neck. In ivory, cream, black, navy, burgundy, emerald, or a soft champagne. Worn tucked into trousers or a skirt, or loose over them.

Why silk for dinner? Because silk catches light. Under candlelight or warm lamps, silk glows. It reflects a soft light onto the face, which is the most flattering thing that can happen to a mature complexion in the evening. It moves when you move, so when you reach for the bread or lift your glass, it ripples gently. And silk feels luxurious, which changes how you sit and how you carry yourself.

If you need warmth, put a fine cashmere layer over it, which we'll come to soon.

Number two. Jeans and sneakers.

Now, I love jeans. And the Italian women I've watched wear jeans beautifully, too, with a blazer, loafers, a silk shirt. But jeans with sneakers, the casual daytime combination, is something they don't wear to dinner.

The reason is the overall message. Jeans and sneakers together say errands, travel, a walk in the park. They're the uniform of the daytime. When you wear them to dinner, you carry the day into the evening with you, and the evening never really begins. Sneakers in particular, chunky and sporty, look out of place under a table set with glasses and linen.

Italian women of a certain age very often choose black wide trousers instead.

Wide-leg trousers in black, in a fabric with some drape, wool crepe, fluid crepe, silk blend, a heavy linen in summer. High-waisted, falling to the floor or just grazing the top of the shoe.

Why black wide trousers? Because they're the easiest elegant garment in the world. They look polished with almost any top. They create a long, unbroken line from waist to floor that's beautiful when you stand and when you walk to the table. They hide everything that a dinner might add to the waistline. And they're comfortable enough to sit in for three hours. Paired with a silk shirt, a knit, or a blazer, they're ready for anything from a family trattoria to a grand restaurant.

And if you love jeans, the women I've watched simply change the shoes and the top. Dark, straight jeans, a silk shirt, a blazer, and loafers or velvet flats can work beautifully for a casual dinner. It's the sneaker that changes the message.

Number three. The sequin top.

Italian women over sixty wear a cashmere wrap instead.

A large, soft cashmere wrap or shawl, in a solid color, camel, black, navy, burgundy, charcoal, ivory, worn over the shoulders, draped around the neck, or wrapped loosely around the upper body.

Why a cashmere wrap? Because it gives you all the luxury and specialness of an evening piece, without any of the noise. Cashmere has a soft, velvety texture that looks rich under warm lighting. Italian women over sixty choose a midi slip skirt instead.

Number six. The shapeless tunic.

Italian women over sixty wear a tailored blazer instead.

A tailored blazer, in wool, crepe, velvet in winter, or linen in summer, in black, navy, camel, charcoal, burgundy, or a soft houndstooth. Fitted at the shoulders, with a slight shape at the waist, falling to the hip.

Let me give you some practical tips for dressing for dinner.

First, change your clothes. Even if it's just your top and your shoes. The act of changing marks the end of the day and the beginning of the evening.

Second, think from the shoulders up. At a table, people see your face, your neck, your shoulders and your hands. Put your best fabric, your best color and your best jewelry there.

Third, choose fabrics that love low light. Silk, cashmere, velvet, wool crepe. They glow under candlelight.

Fourth, choose comfort for sitting. Wide trousers, slip skirts, soft knits. Nothing tight around the waist.

Fifth, keep it quiet. Quiet shoes, quiet jewelry, quiet confidence.

Sixth, carry a layer. Restaurants can be cool, and terraces at night even cooler. A cashmere wrap or a blazer solves this beautifully.

And seventh, finish with your face. A little lipstick in a warm rose, berry or brick, a touch of blush, perhaps a drop of scent at the wrist. The women I have watched at dinner rarely wear heavy makeup, but they almost always have color on their lips, and it ties together the silk, the gold and the candlelight.
