So, who qualifies for Prime Access?

Prime Access is designed for people who receive certain types of government assistance. According to Amazon, qualifying programs include Supplemental Security Income, which most people call SSI. They include Medicaid. They include SNAP, the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program, which is often accessed with an EBT card. They include WIC, the nutrition program for women, infants, and children. They include LIHEAP, the Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program, which helps households pay heating and cooling bills. Amazon's list has also included programs such as TANF, which is Temporary Assistance for Needy Families, the National School Lunch Program, and certain tribal assistance programs. The exact list can change, so the most reliable place to check is Amazon's official Prime Access page, which you can find by going to Amazon's website and searching for Prime Access.

Now, let me draw your attention to a few of those programs, because they are especially relevant to seniors.

First, SSI. Supplemental Security Income is a federal program that provides monthly payments to people with limited income and resources who are sixty-five or older, or who are blind or have a disability. It is different from regular Social Security retirement benefits. Many people get confused here, so let me be very clear. Receiving regular Social Security retirement benefits by itself is not on Amazon's list of qualifying programs. SSI is. If you are not sure which one you receive, check your award letter or your account with the Social Security Administration.

Second, Medicaid. Medicaid is the health coverage program run jointly by the federal government and the states for people with limited income. Many seniors are enrolled in both Medicare and Medicaid at the same time. Those people are sometimes called dual eligible. If you have Medicaid, even alongside Medicare, Medicaid is the program that may qualify you for Prime Access. Medicare by itself is not on Amazon's list.

Third, SNAP and EBT. Many older adults receive SNAP food benefits, and they use an EBT card to buy groceries. If you have an active EBT card for SNAP benefits, that is one of the most common ways people qualify.

Fourth, LIHEAP. This one surprises people. If you received help paying your energy bills through the Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program, you may be able to use that as proof of eligibility.

So if you, your spouse, or a parent you help care for is enrolled in any of these programs, Prime Access could be worth looking into today.

Follow the instructions on screen carefully, and make sure the document is clear and current.

Step three. Wait for confirmation. Sometimes verification happens quickly. Sometimes it takes a little longer, especially if Amazon needs to review your document. Once you are approved, your Prime Access membership begins.

And that brings us to the myth I promised to talk about, because it is one of the most common things we hear from viewers.

Here is the truth. As of this recording, Amazon does not offer a Prime discount based on age alone. There is no AARP Prime discount that I am aware of, and there is no free Prime for seniors simply because they are seniors. Being sixty-five, seventy, or eighty years old does not, by itself, qualify you for a lower price. The discounted program that does exist is Prime Access, and it is based on enrollment in qualifying government assistance programs, not on age.

Why does this myth spread? Partly because many companies do have senior discounts, so people assume Amazon must too. Partly because people hear about Prime Access, which many seniors use, and they mistakenly think it is a senior program. And partly, sadly, because scammers use the idea of a senior discount as bait. If you ever see a message promising an Amazon senior discount, treat it with deep suspicion. The only legitimate discounted Prime membership is the one on Amazon's own website, called Prime Access, and you qualify for it through programs like SSI, Medicaid, SNAP, WIC, or LIHEAP.

There is also a related myth worth clearing up. Some people believe that Amazon offers a special discount for students, and that seniors can somehow get the same deal. Amazon has had a separate program called Prime for Young Adults, aimed at younger people, typically students, with its own rules. That is not available to seniors, and you should not try to sign up for it. Stick with programs you actually qualify for.

Speaking of cancellation, there is some important recent history here. The Federal Trade Commission sued Amazon over the way it signed people up for Prime and the way it made canceling difficult. There is one more change that affects many families. For years, Prime members could share certain shipping benefits with people outside their household through the Prime Invitee program.
