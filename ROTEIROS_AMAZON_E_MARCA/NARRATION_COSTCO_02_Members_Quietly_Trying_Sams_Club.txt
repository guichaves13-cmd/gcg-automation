For decades, Costco loyalists have had a simple rule. You don't shop at Sam's Club. But that rule is quietly breaking. More and more longtime Costco members are walking into Sam's Club, some for the first time in years, and some are even keeping both cards. Why? Because the price gap on membership is real, and Sam's Club has added technology Costco still doesn't match. Tonight we'll compare them honestly, dollar for dollar, and at the end we'll give you a fair verdict on which club actually fits your life.

Let's begin with the number everyone notices first, the membership fee.

On September first, twenty twenty-four, Costco raised its membership fees in the United States and Canada. The basic Gold Star membership went to sixty-five dollars a year. The Executive membership went to one hundred and thirty dollars a year. That was the first increase in about seven years, and it was widely covered in the news.

Sam's Club, which is owned by Walmart, has two main membership levels. The basic Club membership has been priced at fifty dollars a year. The upgraded Plus membership has been priced at one hundred and ten dollars a year. Those are the standard prices, and Sam's Club has a long history of running promotions that can bring the first-year cost down even further, sometimes dramatically. Promotions come and go, so check the current offer before you sign up.

So right away, at the basic level, Sam's Club is about fifteen dollars a year cheaper than Costco. At the premium level, it's about twenty dollars a year cheaper. That's not a fortune. But for a retiree on a fixed income, it's not nothing either. And when people start comparing fees, they often start comparing everything else, too.

Let's talk about what you get at the premium level, because that's where a lot of the comparison happens.

Costco's Executive membership gives you a two percent annual reward on most qualifying purchases, with a cap that rose from one thousand dollars to one thousand two hundred and fifty dollars a year in that twenty twenty-four change. Starting June thirtieth, twenty twenty-five, Executive members also got special early shopping hours, about an hour before regular opening at most warehouses.

Sam's Club Plus has its own package. Plus members have generally earned rewards called Sam's Cash on qualifying purchases, at a rate that has been around two percent, with an annual cap that has been lower than Costco's. Plus members have also generally received early shopping hours, free shipping on many items ordered online, and certain pharmacy and optical savings. The exact terms of Sam's Club Plus have changed several times over the years, so treat these as general descriptions and confirm the current details with Sam's Club directly.

What that means is this. If you're a very high spender, Costco's higher reward cap may favor Costco. If you're a moderate spender, both programs can pay back a similar percentage, and Sam's Club costs a bit less to join.

Now let's get to the shelves, because a membership is only as good as what you buy with it.

Both clubs are built around their store brands. At Costco, it's Kirkland Signature. At Sam's Club, it's Member's Mark. These two brands are the heart of each store, and they're where many shoppers form their loyalty.

Kirkland Signature has a remarkable reputation. Costco has said it represents a large share of its sales, and many shoppers believe Kirkland products match or beat name brands on quality. Think of Kirkland coffee, Kirkland olive oil, Kirkland paper towels, Kirkland batteries, and Kirkland vitamins. It's become one of the most recognized store brands in America.

Member's Mark has historically had a weaker reputation. For years, some shoppers saw it as a step below Kirkland. But Sam's Club has worked hard to change that. In recent years, Sam's Club has publicly talked about reformulating and upgrading many Member's Mark products, adding new items, and improving packaging. Whether those changes have closed the gap is a matter of personal taste, and reviews from shoppers are mixed. Some people now say they prefer certain Member's Mark items, while others still swear by Kirkland.

Here's the honest advice. Don't take anybody's word for it, including ours. Make a short list of the five or ten store-brand items you buy most often. Paper towels, toilet paper, coffee, trash bags, frozen vegetables, nuts, laundry detergent, whatever your staples are. Then compare the unit price, the price per ounce or per sheet, at both stores. Shelf prices change and vary by region, so the only comparison that matters is the one you do at your own local stores. You may find that one club is cheaper on your coffee and the other is cheaper on your paper goods.

Now we come to the feature that has pulled many Costco members across the parking lot. Scan and Go.

Sam's Club Scan and Go lets you use the Sam's Club app on your smartphone to scan items as you put them in your cart. When you're done, you pay right in the app, and you skip the checkout line entirely. Sam's Club made Scan and Go available to its members years ago, and it has become one of the club's signature features.

More recently, Sam's Club has rolled out exit technology at many of its clubs that uses cameras and computer vision to verify your purchase as you leave, instead of having an employee check your receipt by hand. The idea is to make the whole trip faster, from scanning to paying to walking out the door. Sam's Club has said it plans to bring this technology to its clubs nationwide.

For a lot of people, that's a big deal. Anyone who has stood in a long Costco checkout line on a Saturday afternoon, and then stood in a second line to have the receipt checked at the door, understands the appeal.

Costco, to be fair, has been working on its own technology. Costco has added self-checkout lanes at many warehouses. In twenty twenty-four, it rolled out membership card scanners at entrances, which mainly help confirm that shoppers are actual members. And Costco has talked about testing ways to speed up checkout. But as of now, Costco does not offer a full Scan and Go system that matches what Sam's Club offers. If skipping the line is your top priority, Sam's Club currently has the edge.

There is one caution here, especially for older shoppers. Scan and Go depends on your smartphone, an app, and a payment method stored in that app. If you're comfortable with your phone, it's easy. If you're not, it may feel like more trouble than it's worth. Sam's Club still has regular checkout lanes, so you're never forced to use the app. But the full benefit really comes to people who are comfortable with the technology.

Before we go further, here's something important. In a few minutes, we're going to cover the areas where Costco still clearly wins, and there are several. If you're thinking about switching, you'll want to hear that part before you make any decision. So stay with us.

Next, let's talk about curbside pickup and delivery.

Sam's Club offers curbside pickup at its clubs. You place your order in the app or online, you drive to the club, and an employee brings your order out to your car. Depending on your membership level and the current terms, there may be a fee for Club members, while Plus members have generally received free curbside pickup. For retirees who don't want to push a heavy flatbed cart across a parking lot, or who have trouble lifting cases of water, that can be a genuine quality-of-life improvement.

Costco's approach is different. Costco has not built a broad curbside pickup program at its warehouses the way Sam's Club has. Instead, Costco offers same-day delivery in many areas through a partnership with Instacart. That's convenient, but it's important to know something. Prices for same-day delivery items through that service are often higher than the prices you'd pay in the warehouse. Costco and Instacart have disclosed that same-day prices may be marked up, and there can also be delivery fees and tips on top of that. So if you rely on same-day delivery from Costco, you may be giving back a good part of the savings that make Costco worthwhile in the first place.

Costco also sells many items through Costco dot com, and for very large orders, there's Costco Business Delivery in many areas. And there's a lesser-known program called Costco Next, which is an online marketplace on Costco's website featuring selected brands and products shipped directly from those companies, with member pricing. These options are useful, but none of them is quite the same as walking out of the house, driving to the club, and having your order loaded into your trunk.

So if curbside pickup matters to you, especially if you have limited mobility, Sam's Club currently offers a more complete version of that service.

Let's talk about size and convenience, because location matters a lot.

Costco has more than nine hundred warehouses worldwide, with roughly six hundred of them in the United States. Sam's Club has around six hundred clubs, all in the United States. So in terms of U.S. locations, the two are in the same general ballpark. But where they're located is very different. Some regions are full of Costcos and have few Sam's Clubs, and other regions are the reverse. In many smaller towns and rural areas, a Sam's Club may be much closer than the nearest Costco. If the nearest Costco is forty minutes away and a Sam's Club is ten minutes away, the extra drive time and gas can easily outweigh small price differences.

Now, let's turn to where Costco still wins, because it wins in several important ways.

First, the return policy. Costco has one of the most generous return policies in American retail. For most items, Costco does not set a strict time limit on returns if you're not satisfied, though there are exceptions. For example, most electronics and major appliances generally have a ninety-day return window. Costco also has a long-standing policy of refunding your membership fee if you're dissatisfied. Sam's Club also has a generous return policy, and it also offers membership refunds under its own terms, but Costco's reputation in this area is especially strong and is a major reason many members stay loyal.

Second, Kirkland Signature. As we discussed, Member's Mark has improved, but Kirkland's reputation is still a powerful advantage. Many shoppers trust Kirkland in a way that took decades to build.

Third, the higher reward cap for big spenders. If you spend heavily, Costco's Executive reward cap of one thousand two hundred and fifty dollars can give you more total money back than Sam's Club Plus, depending on its current cap.

Fourth, gasoline. Costco gas stations are known for competitive prices, and they're very popular. In twenty twenty-five, Costco also adjusted gas station hours, including extended hours for Executive members at many locations. Sam's Club also sells gas at many clubs, often at competitive prices as well. Which is cheaper in your area depends entirely on your local stations, so compare them.

Fifth, the overall shopping experience and the treasure hunt. Many members simply enjoy shopping at Costco. The rotating seasonal items, the jewelry case, the big furniture displays, the bakery, and of course the famous food court with its long-running hot dog and soda combo. For a lot of people, a Costco trip is an outing. That's hard to put a price on.

Sixth, travel and services. Costco Travel has a strong following among members who book vacations, cruises, and rental cars through it. Sam's Club has its own travel and service offerings as well, but many Costco members value Costco Travel highly.

And seventh, international reach. If you travel internationally, a Costco membership can generally be used at Costco warehouses in other countries. Sam's Club's clubs are in the United States.

Now, where does Sam's Club win? Let's be just as fair.

Sam's Club wins on membership price. It's cheaper at both levels, and it frequently runs promotions for new members.

Sam's Club wins on checkout technology, with Scan and Go and its newer exit technology.

Sam's Club generally wins on curbside pickup, which Costco doesn't broadly offer.

Sam's Club may win on convenience in areas where it has more locations nearby.

And for some households, Sam's Club may win on specific product prices, especially when it runs instant savings on items you buy anyway. The only way to know is to compare.

So why are some Costco members quietly trying Sam's Club? For many, it's not about abandoning Costco. It's about curiosity, convenience, and value. Some people sign up during a promotional deal, try it for a year, and see what they think. Some find that the curbside pickup or the Scan and Go makes their life easier. Some live closer to a Sam's Club. And some decide to keep both memberships, using each club for what it does best.

That two-membership strategy is worth thinking about. If you're paying sixty-five dollars for Costco and fifty dollars for a basic Sam's Club membership, that's one hundred and fifteen dollars a year total. That's actually less than a single Costco Executive membership at one hundred and thirty. For some shoppers, having two basic memberships gives them more flexibility than one premium membership. You could buy your Kirkland favorites and gas at Costco, and use Sam's Club for curbside pickups and quick trips with Scan and Go. Whether that makes sense depends on how often you shop and how far you have to drive.

But there's a caution here too. Two memberships only make sense if you use both. If one card sits in your wallet unused, you're wasting money. Before you add a second club, be honest about whether you'll actually shop there.

Here's a simple approach if you're curious. First, compare your top ten staples at both stores, by unit price. Second, think about the features you'd actually use. Do you want curbside pickup? Will you really use Scan and Go? Do you care about early shopping hours? Third, consider the drive. Fourth, if you're still undecided, watch for a promotional deal on a Sam's Club membership, and try it for a year. Both clubs offer refunds on membership fees under their own policies if you're dissatisfied, so ask about the terms before you join.

And if you're currently a Costco Executive member, you might also consider whether you actually need the Executive level. As we've covered on this channel, the Executive upgrade costs sixty-five dollars more than Gold Star, so you need to spend about three thousand two hundred and fifty dollars a year on qualifying purchases for the two percent reward to cover it. If you're below that, the money you save by downgrading might be enough to pay for a basic Sam's Club membership, giving you access to both clubs for less than you pay now.

Let's also walk through what a typical trip might look like at each club, because the experience is part of the value.

Picture a Tuesday morning at Costco. You park, you walk to the entrance, and you scan your membership card or the digital card in your app. You grab a cart and start in the center aisles, where the seasonal items and the electronics are. You work your way to the back for the meat and dairy, pick up a rotisserie chicken, maybe a pumpkin pie if it's the season, and head to the registers. You load everything onto the belt, an employee rings you up, you pay, and on the way out someone checks your receipt. For many people, that rhythm is comfortable and familiar, and on a quiet weekday it moves quickly.

Now picture the same Tuesday at a Sam's Club that has the newer technology. You open the app before you walk in. As you shop, you scan each item's barcode with your phone and drop it in the cart. The app keeps a running total, which some shoppers on a budget find very helpful, because you know exactly what you're spending before you reach the front. When you're done, you tap to pay in the app, and you head for the door. At clubs with the newer exit system, you may simply walk out without a receipt check. If you'd rather not shop at all that day, you could have placed the order the night before and picked it up at the curb.

Neither experience is wrong. They're just different. One is built around a traditional store visit. The other is built around your phone.

There are other departments worth comparing, especially for older shoppers. Both clubs have pharmacies, and both have generally allowed people to use the pharmacy even without a membership in many states, though rules can vary by location and by law. Prescription prices can differ, and both clubs have offered discount programs for members. If you take regular medications, it's worth asking both pharmacies for a price quote, and comparing that to what you pay through your insurance or Medicare plan. Sometimes the difference is meaningful.

Both clubs also have optical centers, and both have hearing aid centers at many locations. Costco's hearing aid centers in particular have a strong following among retirees, and Costco has been known for competitive pricing on hearing aids. Sam's Club also offers hearing aids and hearing tests at many clubs. If you or your spouse are considering hearing aids, which can cost thousands of dollars elsewhere, comparing these two options could save you far more than the difference in membership fees. Ask about warranties, follow-up visits, and return periods, because those details matter as much as the price.

Then there's tires and auto service. Both clubs run tire centers at many locations, with installation packages and periodic promotions. Costco's tire program is popular, and Sam's Club also sells tires with its own installation packages. Again, compare a quote for your specific car and tire size, because prices and promotions shift throughout the year.

And don't forget the bakery and fresh foods. Some shoppers are fiercely loyal to Costco's bakery items and its meat department. Others have discovered that they like the Sam's Club bakery or certain Member's Mark fresh items just as well. Fresh food is where personal taste matters most, so this is one area where a trial visit really pays off.

So now, the verdict. And we'll be fair about it.

If you're a heavy spender who loves Kirkland products, values Costco's return policy, uses Costco Travel, and lives near a Costco, there's little reason to leave. Costco remains an excellent value, and its high renewal rate, roughly ninety percent in the United States and Canada according to the company, reflects that.

If you're a smaller household, want curbside pickup, enjoy using your smartphone to skip the checkout line, or live closer to a Sam's Club, it's well worth trying Sam's Club, especially if you can get a promotional rate.

And if you're somewhere in between, the two-card strategy may give you the best of both worlds, as long as you'll truly use both.

There's no single right answer. The best warehouse club is the one that saves you the most money and the most hassle, based on your own habits and your own neighborhood. Not the one your neighbor swears by, and not the one with the slickest advertising.
