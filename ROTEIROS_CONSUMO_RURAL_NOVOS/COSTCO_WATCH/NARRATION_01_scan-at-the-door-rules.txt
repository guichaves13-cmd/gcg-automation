Let's start at the front door. For decades, the Costco entrance had a very simple routine. You walked in, an employee glanced at your card, maybe you flashed it from three feet away, and you were through. Picture a crowded Saturday morning, a cart corral, a greeter in a vest standing beside a podium, and dozens of people holding up cards with their thumbs over the photo. Honestly, a lot of people got in without anyone looking closely at all.

That changed. In 2024 Costco began testing membership scanners at warehouse entrances, and over the following year it rolled them out widely across U.S. locations. Now, instead of a quick glance, you tap or scan your physical card, or the digital membership card in the Costco app, on a small pedestal-style reader. Picture a waist-high black stand with a glowing scanner window, a member holding a phone over it, and a small green confirmation light before they push their cart forward.

The first open loop: why? The obvious answer is fraud. People sharing cards, borrowing a friend's membership, walking in behind someone else. And yes, Costco has been open about wanting to stop membership sharing. In 2023 it said publicly that it had noticed non-members using other people's cards, particularly at self-checkout, and it began requiring photo membership cards at self-checkout lanes. But I'll explain later why the scanner at the door is about more than just catching freeloaders. There's a business number behind it that matters a lot more to the company than a few borrowed cards.

Before that, though, let's get the basic rules clear, because confusion at the door is creating real friction for members.

Rule one: every adult entering is expected to have a valid membership or be accompanied by a member. Costco's long-standing policy lets a member bring guests, typically up to two, though only the member can make purchases, with some exceptions for gift cards. That hasn't fundamentally changed, but with the scanners, the member now actually has to scan in, and guests walk through with them.

Rule two: your card has to be active. If your membership expired last month and you forgot to renew, the scanner is going to tell on you. Rule three: the digital card counts. If you've downloaded the Costco app and signed in, your digital membership card works at the scanner. For a lot of people this is the easiest option, because nobody wants to dig through a wallet with a toddler in one arm and a cart in the other.

Now let's talk about the Executive membership, because this is the change that makes the math interesting, and it's the second open loop I promised.

In September 2024, Costco raised its U.S. and Canadian membership fees for the first time since 2017. Gold Star and Business went from sixty to sixty-five dollars. To hit the full one-thousand-two-hundred-and-fifty-dollar reward, you'd need to spend sixty-two thousand five hundred dollars a year on qualifying purchases. That's a lot of groceries, but it's not unusual for small businesses, restaurants, or large households that also buy things like appliances, tires, and electronics at Costco. For those shoppers, the new cap is worth an extra two hundred and fifty dollars a year compared to the old one. That more than covers the fee increase on its own.

So the Executive membership got more expensive, but for heavy spenders it also got more valuable. For light shoppers, honestly, it might not be worth it, especially because Costco has long offered a satisfaction guarantee on membership that includes the option to downgrade, and its terms say it will refund the difference if your reward doesn't cover the upgrade cost. Now, here's the full answer to our first open loop. Why scan at the door?

Yes, it's about stopping membership sharing. But the deeper reason is that membership fees are the heart of Costco's business. According to Costco's financial filings, membership fees bring in several billion dollars a year, and they account for a very large share of the company's operating profit. The merchandise itself is sold on thin margins on purpose. That's the whole Costco model: keep prices low, and earn a big chunk of the profit from the annual fee. So every person who shops on a borrowed card is not a small loss. They're someone who would otherwise have to pay sixty-five or one hundred and thirty dollars for the privilege.

In 2024, after the company began enforcing card checks more strictly, Costco executives said on earnings calls that the crackdown on membership sharing had contributed to new sign-ups. In other words, tightening the doors didn't just stop free riders. It turned some of them into paying members. That's the real reason the scanner exists. It protects the single most important number in Costco's business.

And it gives Costco something else too: data. A scanner knows exactly when each member arrives and at which warehouse. Here's the mistake. A lot of Executive members assume their gas and food court spending is building their reward, and they upgrade based on a total spending number that includes fuel. Let me also clear up a few questions I keep hearing about the new door rules, because there's a lot of confusion floating around online.

First, can a non-member still get in with a Costco Shop Card? Costco has long said that Shop Card holders who aren't members can use the card to shop, but in practice how this works at the scanner can vary by warehouse, and some members report being asked to visit the membership counter first. If you're giving someone a Shop Card as a gift, tell them to bring it and expect a conversation at the door.

Second, what about the pharmacy, the optical center and the hearing aid center? In many U.S. states, pharmacy laws allow non-members to fill prescriptions at Costco, and the company has said for years that you don't need a membership to use the pharmacy. Picture the pharmacy counter tucked into a back corner past the vitamins, with a small sign that says membership not required for pharmacy. The door staff are supposed to let you through for that purpose, so just say so when you arrive.

Third, what about buying alcohol? In some states, liquor laws require Costco to sell alcohol to anyone of legal age, member or not. Some Costco liquor stores sit in separate storefronts next to the warehouse for exactly this reason. Picture a small shopfront with its own door and its own sign, just a few steps from the main entrance.

And fourth, what about the household card? A Costco membership typically includes one free household card for someone over eighteen living at the same address. That person gets their own photo card and can scan in on their own. If you've been sharing a single card with a partner, this is the moment to sign them up properly. It's free, and it saves you both the awkward moment at the scanner.

The bottom line on all four: the scanner isn't designed to keep out people with a legitimate reason to be there. It's designed to stop people using memberships that aren't theirs.
